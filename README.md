## About

Financial fraud in the federal system encompasses a wide range of schemes that defraud investors, banks, and the public. From small-scale embezzlement to massive Ponzi schemes, the DOJ prosecutes financial fraud across all 94 federal districts.

## Major Categories of Federal Financial Fraud

### Ponzi Schemes
Named after Charles Ponzi, these schemes pay early investors using funds from new investors rather than actual investment returns. Bernie Madoff's $65 billion scheme is the largest Ponzi case in history. Ponzi cases are prosecuted under securities fraud, wire fraud, and mail fraud statutes.

### Securities Fraud
Securities fraud includes insider trading (trading on material non-public information), market manipulation, pump-and-dump schemes, and accounting fraud at publicly traded companies.

### Bank Fraud
Fraudulent schemes targeting financial institutions, including loan fraud, check fraud, account takeovers, and fraudulent account opening.

### Wire Fraud
The wire fraud statute (18 U.S.C. § 1343) is the most versatile tool in the federal fraud arsenal — any scheme to defraud using electronic communications. Wire fraud is used to prosecute everything from email scams to complex financial fraud.

### Mortgage Fraud
False statements on mortgage applications, fraudulent appraisals, title company fraud, and straw buyer schemes targeting banks and borrowers.

### Cryptocurrency Fraud
NFT fraud, exchange manipulation, DeFi rug pulls, and other cryptocurrency fraud schemes increasingly dominate financial fraud dockets.

## Investigating Financial Fraud

Financial fraud investigations rely heavily on:
- **Financial records**: Bank records, investment accounts, tax returns
- **Email and communications**: Electronic communications often reveal fraud
- **Witness cooperation**: Participants who cooperate with prosecutors
- **Expert witnesses**: CPAs and financial experts to explain complex schemes
- **Asset tracing**: Following money to identify fraud proceeds

## Asset Forfeiture

Federal law allows forfeiture of property involved in financial fraud:
- Direct proceeds of crime
- Property used to facilitate crime
- Property involved in money laundering

Forfeiture is both criminal (during prosecution) and civil (independent of prosecution).

## Restitution to Victims

Federal fraud convictions typically require restitution to victims under the Mandatory Victims Restitution Act. Courts must order full restitution regardless of ability to pay. Collecting restitution from defendants who've lost or spent fraud proceeds is often difficult.
